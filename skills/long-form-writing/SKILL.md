# Long-form writing

Long texts fail in the structure long before they fail in the sentences. Settle who it's for and
what it must do, shape it, then draft and revise in separate passes.

## 1. Reader and point

- Who reads it, what they already know, and what they want from it. Write for one specific reader.
- The point: what the reader should know, feel or do afterwards, in one or two sentences. For
  fiction, what the piece is about underneath its plot.
- Constraints: length, tone, format, deadline, the words the project uses (its glossary), and
  anything that must or must not be said.

## 2. Material

- Gather before you write: facts with their sources, examples, quotes, scenes, data. Note where
  each came from.
- Mark what you don't know yet. Don't paper over a gap with vague sentences; find it out or say it
  is open.

## 3. Structure

- Outline in a few lines, one per section, each saying what that section does for the reader
  (sets up the problem, shows the evidence, turns the argument, resolves the conflict).
- Order by what the reader needs next, not by the order you found things out.
- Every section should earn its place. If you can't say what it does, cut it or merge it.
- For stories: know the change from beginning to end, and where the turns are.

## 4. Draft

- Draft fast, section by section, following the outline. Don't polish while drafting.
- Lead with the point in each section; put details after.
- Use concrete nouns and specific examples instead of abstractions.
- Keep the project's terms consistent: one word per thing.

## 5. Revise in passes

Do one thing per pass:

1. **Structure.** Does each section do its job? Is anything missing, repeated or in the wrong
   place?
2. **Paragraphs.** Does each open with its point? Does each follow from the last?
3. **Sentences.** Cut words that don't carry meaning. Prefer short sentences and active verbs, and
   replace jargon the reader doesn't share.
4. **Voice.** Read it aloud, or as the reader would. Remove filler, hedging and stock phrases.
5. **Facts.** Check every name, number, date and quote against its source.

## 6. Hand it over

Say what you were aiming for, what you're unsure about, and what you'd like checked: facts, tone,
length or one specific section. A reviewer reading for "anything" finds less than one reading for
something.
