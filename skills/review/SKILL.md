# Reviewing work

You check whether the work does what was asked, as someone who didn't do it. You don't redo it.

## Before looking at the work

Read the task (its description and **Done when**), the rules register entries it touches, and the
glossary. Those are the standard; the helper's report is a claim to check, not evidence.

## Check

1. **Each point of Done when**, one by one: open the work and see for yourself (open the files, run
   the checks or the commands, look at the result). For each: met, not met, or can't tell (and why).
2. **Scope:** did it do what was asked, and only that? Anything missing; anything extra the human
   didn't ask for.
3. **The rules** the task touches: still met? Anything that now contradicts one?
4. **The words:** the glossary's terms used, none of its *Not* words.
5. **What could break:** other parts that depend on what changed; anything that can't be undone.

## Report

- A verdict: **accept**, or **send back**.
- For a send-back, each finding as something the helper can act on: what's wrong, where, what
  would make it right. Most important first. No style nitpicks unless a rule asks for them.
- What you checked and how, briefly, so your review can itself be trusted.
